## Behavioral question answer
<details>
  <summary><b>1. Can you describe a time when you had to learn something new quickly? How did you approach it?</b></summary>
  <h3>Situation</h3>
<p>"When I started my graduate research assistantship at UNT, I was assigned to build an AI-powered query system for Friction Stir Additive Manufacturing research — FSAM. I had a computer science background but FSAM is a materials science field I had never encountered before."</P>

<h3>Task</h3>
<p>"My task was to build a system that could answer natural language questions about FSAM research. But to do that effectively, I first needed to understand the domain — the terminology, the research questions, what mattered to scientists in that field."</P>

<h3>Action</h3>
<p>"I took a three-step approach. First, I connected directly with the materials science department — I met with Professor Mishra and his students to understand what questions they actually needed answered and what data existed. Second, I read research papers in the field to understand the core concepts — alloy types, process parameters, mechanical properties. Third, I researched online resources and existing FSAM datasets to understand the data structure before building the ingestion pipeline."</P>

<h3>Result</h3>
<p>"Within a few weeks I had enough domain knowledge to design the right system architecture — a hybrid SQL plus vector retrieval approach that matched how scientists actually ask questions. The final system successfully queried across 57 peer-reviewed papers and reduced literature review time from days to seconds for the research team."</p>
  
<h3> Conclusion </h3>
<p>"That experience taught me that when learning something new, going directly to the experts and combining that with self-study is always faster than trying to figure it out alone."</P>
